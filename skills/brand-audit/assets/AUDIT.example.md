# Visual landscape audit: rules for agents

You are designing a brand that enters this category. Read this file fully.
Flash mode: every classification below is a hypothesis from 8 direct brands.
Counts are `n of base`. Each rule ends with the evidence it rests on.

## 0. Frame

Ordering and stock app for independent bookshops. Bought by owners of one to three shops.
Today they use distributor portals and a paper order book.

## 1. Preserve: category codes

Drop one of these and a shop owner no longer recognises what is being sold.

1. Show the product screen on the home page. 6 of 8 do; it is how the category is recognised when owners compare tools. `evidence/sheets/heroes.webp`

## 2. Avoid: clichés, and what belongs to a competitor

Clichés are dominant and proven breakable: an established brand does otherwise and is still read as the category.

2. Do not use blue as the brand colour. 6 of 8 are blue; Bindery, established, uses none. `evidence/sheets/palettes.webp` `evidence/bindery/desktop.webp`
3. Do not put a pictogram next to the name by default. 7 of 8 are combination marks; Bindery is a bare wordmark. `evidence/sheets/logos.webp` `evidence/bindery/logo.webp`
4. Never an open book as the symbol. 4 of 8 use one. `evidence/sheets/logos.webp`
5. A single accent hue is a default, not a code. 6 of 8; Libra Order, the oldest brand, runs two. `evidence/libra/desktop.webp`
6. Do not open on a benefit line. 5 of 8 do; Bindery opens on a manifesto, Libra Order on a proof. `evidence/sheets/heroes.webp`
7. **Owned by Folio:** royal blue with a white open book. A new brand in that pairing is read as a Folio product. `evidence/folio/desktop.webp` `evidence/folio/logo.webp`

## 3. Challenge: risky breaks and emerging codes

Nobody established breaks these, and they tell the buyer nothing. Either the best opening or a trap: decide on purpose.

8. A light ground. 8 of 8. Atelier Paper shows a coloured ground working for the same shopkeepers in stationery. `evidence/atelier-paper/desktop.webp`
9. A pictorial symbol. 5 of 7 combination marks. **Emerging:** the two brands launched since 2022 chose an abstract mark and a letter. `evidence/stacks/logo.webp` `evidence/marque/logo.webp`
10. A mark that sits still. 8 of 8 are static and upright. `evidence/sheets/logos.webp`

## 4. Explore: open territories

Ranked. Each one keeps rule 1 and says what it breaks.

11. **The order book.** Keep the product screen. Break blue and the book pictogram. Borrow from Atelier Paper: a paper-coloured ground and a serif wordmark. The thing owners use today is a ruled paper book; that territory is empty in the panel. Risk: medium. `evidence/atelier-paper/desktop.webp`
12. **The back office.** Keep the product screen and the light ground. Break blue and the split hero. Borrow from Ledgerly: dense grids and a mono label system. Everyone sells ease; nobody shows the density owners work in. Risk: low. `evidence/ledgerly/desktop.webp`

## 5. Validate

The audit reads captures. It does not know how people perceive them.

- Whether a paper-toned brand reads as a serious tool or as nostalgia: show both versions to five shop owners.
- Whether density on the home page puts off first-time buyers.
- That the chosen name and sign are legally available.

## 6. For brand-kit

- **Swap test competitor:** Folio. Largest installed base and the most typical of the category. `evidence/folio/desktop.webp`
- **Category duel:** most typical `evidence/folio/desktop.webp` against most deviant `evidence/bindery/desktop.webp`.
- **Seed for the Not list:** blue SaaS accent, open-book pictogram, split hero with a floating dashboard, "save time" benefit headline.
