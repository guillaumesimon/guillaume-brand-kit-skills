## 0. Brief

Ordering and stock app for independent bookshops. For: Owners of one to three shops. Market: United Kingdom.
Used today instead: Distributor portals and a paper order book.

## 1. Thesis

Be read at once as a tool for running a bookshop, and as nothing like the blue software brands that sell it today: closer to the paper order book owners already trust.

**Central tension.** Recognition as software against the warmth and craft of the trade it serves.

**Most consequential uncertainty.** Whether shop owners read a paper-toned brand as a serious tool or as nostalgia. Nothing in the captures can tell.

## 2. Decisions

### Required

- **D-01 · Imagery.** Show the product screen in the first view. It is how owners recognise the category when they compare tools. Trade-off: Less room for an image of the shop. Confidence: medium. Rests on F-VIS-01, C-06.
- **D-02 · Logo.** A mark that reads at 16 px. The product lives in a browser tab. Trade-off: Rules out fine detail. Confidence: high. Rests on C-13.

### Explore

- **D-03 · Colour.** A warm or paper-toned ground. Six of eight are blue and no warm hue appears in the panel. Trade-off: May read as stationery rather than software. Confidence: medium. Rests on F-COL-01, F-COL-02, C-03.

### Deprioritise

- **D-04 · Symbolism.** The open book. Four of eight symbols already are one. Trade-off: Gives up the fastest way to say "books". Confidence: high. Rests on F-SYM-01.

### Avoid

- **D-05 · Colour.** Royal blue with a white open book. That pairing is Folio's. Trade-off: None worth keeping. Confidence: high. Rests on C-11.

## 4. Creative territories

Alternative answers to the same brief. Hypotheses to explore, not directions to execute.

### 1. The order book (T-order-book)

*The tool that replaces the paper book looks like it learned from it.* A ruled, warm, typographic brand that an owner recognises as their own trade.

Intended perception: Made by people who know a shop floor.

- personality: Careful, plain-spoken, a little bookish
- logo: A serif wordmark with one detachable device
- symbolism: The ruled line, the tally, the margin note
- colour: A paper ground and one ink
- typography: A serif for the voice, a mono for figures
- photography: The shop and its counter, in daylight
- graphic system: Hairline rules and index labels
- voice: Statements, not benefits

Keeps: the product screen in the first view; a mark that reads at 16 px. Challenges: blue; the book pictogram; the white page.
Risk: May read as nostalgic or as stationery. Confidence: medium. To validate: Show it beside Folio to five owners and ask which one they would trust with their stock.
Left open: The exact ground tone; Which device detaches from the wordmark; How much of the shop to show.
Rests on F-COL-02, F-SYM-01, C-03.

### 2. The back office (T-back-office)

*The serious tool shows its density instead of hiding it.* A calm, tabular brand for people who count stock all day.

Intended perception: The professional choice.

- personality: Exact, unhurried, dry
- logo: A lettermark or a compact wordmark
- symbolism: The grid, the column, the count
- colour: Black on white with one functional accent
- typography: A mono label system over a neutral text face
- photography: None: the interface is the picture
- graphic system: Dense grids, aligned figures
- voice: How it works, in few words

Keeps: the product screen in the first view; a light ground. Challenges: blue; the benefit headline; the floating dashboard.
Risk: May put off a first-time buyer. Confidence: medium. To validate: Whether a dense first screen loses sign-ups against a simplified one.
Left open: The accent and what it means; How dense is too dense on a phone.
Rests on F-VIS-01, F-TYP-01.

Moodboard, one mechanism per image:

## 5. References: borrow the mechanism, not the look

- `evidence/atelier-paper/desktop.webp` Mechanism: One ground tone carries the whole identity. Reinterpret: Find the ground that belongs to a bookshop counter, not to a stationer. Do not copy: Its brown, its serif, its layout.
- `evidence/ledgerly/desktop.webp` Mechanism: A mono label system makes dense data feel ordered. Reinterpret: Labels that name what a bookseller counts. Do not copy: Its grid proportions and lettermark.

## 6. Judging a concept

A gate rejects a concept. A preference weighs on it.

- **Functional** (gate): The mark is recognisable at 16 px in a browser tab, in one colour.
- **Category recognition** (gate): A shop owner says what the product is within five seconds of seeing the home page.
- **Differentiation** (preference): Placed in the logo wall, it is not mistaken for any of the eight.
- **System** (preference): Favicon, home page and product screen are recognised as one brand with the logo covered.
