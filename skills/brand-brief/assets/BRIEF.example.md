## 0. In short

Perch gives the people who run an independent hotel the calm of knowing every room is handled, without turning the hotel into a control room.

- For owner-managers of 20 to 80 room hotels who run housekeeping and maintenance on paper and a group chat.
- It should feel like a well-kept ledger: quiet, exact, on the side of the staff.
- Competent without being corporate, warm without being cute, and never a dashboard that watches people.
- It must work as a 16 px app icon on a housekeeper's phone and embroidered in one colour on a uniform.

## 1. Business context

- **Product.** A phone app and a front-desk screen that tell every member of a hotel's staff which rooms and repairs are theirs today, and tell the manager what is done. _(documented: README.md)_
- **Reason to exist.** Independent hotels lose rooms and reviews to things nobody was told about: a broken shower reported on paper, a late checkout that never reached housekeeping. _(stated: q01)_ "Nobody is lazy in a hotel. Things fall between two people, that's all."
- **Problem with what exists.** The tools sold to hotels are built for chains: long set-up, a manager's dashboard first, staff treated as rows. _(stated: q02)_
- **Used today instead.** A paper list at reception and a WhatsApp group. The paper is faster to write and everyone already knows how. _(stated: q02)_ "The paper never needs a login."
- **Difference.** Built for the housekeeper's first minute, not the manager's first report: a new member of staff uses it without training. _(stated: q03)_
- **Reason to believe.** No structural reason to believe yet. Two pilot hotels onboarded their teams in a morning, which is an anecdote, not a proof. _(inferred: q03)_

## 2. Audiences

- **Owner-managers of independent hotels (primary).** 20 to 80 rooms, often family-run, the manager also covers reception. Needs: To stop being the only memory of the building. Should feel: Relieved: I can go home. _(stated: q04)_ "I want to be able to leave at six without my phone ringing at seven."
- **Housekeeping and maintenance staff.** Often not native English speakers, on their own phones. Needs: To know what is theirs today without being watched. Should feel: Respected. _(inferred: q04, q06)_

## 3. Positioning

- **Compared today with.** Hotel management software, next to property management systems. _(stated: q02)_
- **Wants to be compared with.** The hotel's own logbook, next to the paper it replaces. _(chosen: q05)_
- **Promise.** Nothing in the building falls between two people. _(inferred: q01, q05)_

**Territory chosen: The well-kept ledger.** Quiet and exact. The hotel's own book, kept properly.
- Kept from "The colleague who never forgets": on the side of the staff, speaks plainly
- Rejected: "The control tower" (Everything visible at once. The manager sees all.) It is what the staff fear, and what every competitor sells.
- Not ruled on: "The modern challenger"

## 4. Ambition

- **Horizon.** In five years, the default for independent hotels in the UK and Ireland, then French and Italian family hotels. _(stated: q07)_
- **Scale.** A product company of thirty people, not a platform. _(stated: q07)_
- **The brand must be able to carry.** The brand has to survive translation into French and Italian and a second product for small restaurants. _(inferred: q07)_

## 8. Creative tensions

- **Exact without being cold** (Competence / closeness). The limit is crossed when: A screen could belong to a bank, or a sentence could not be said aloud at the front desk.
- **Restrained without being anonymous** (Quiet / distinctive). The limit is crossed when: The work is not recognisable once the logo is covered.

## 9. Creative preferences

Signals from what the founder chose and refused. They are taste, not rules, except Must and Never.

### Must

### Preference

- Print discipline: rules, small labels, real tables. _(chosen: r1-03, r2-02)_

### To explore

### Never

- No mascot and no illustrated characters. _(chosen: r1-04)_
- No dashboard as the first image of the brand. _(chosen: r1-02, q06)_

**Founder's taste against the audience's need.** The founder is drawn to very quiet, print-like references. Risk: Housekeeping staff on a small phone need large, obvious controls; a magazine's restraint can read as hard to use. Arbitration: Quiet on the brand's surfaces, generous and plain in the product. Decided by the founder.

## 11. Creative principles

1. **If it needs explaining, it is not finished.** No training is a value the founder has paid for. _(q06)_
2. **Show the building, not the dashboard.** The dashboard is the category's first image and what the staff fear. _(r1-02, q06)_
3. **Say it the way it would be said at the front desk.** Plain speech was chosen every time it was offered. _(r1-01, r1-05)_
