Illustration styles

The subject says what you do; the style says who you are. Two illustrations of an identical scene leave entirely different impressions depending on how they are drawn — so here the scene is held constant and only the drawing changes.

Which two could be yours? Then: which one is definitely not?
Your tone
Structured, authoritativeWarm, playful

Realistic Isometric and Playful Editorial 3D sit at the structured end. Clever Doodle and New School Cartoon sit at the warm end. Playful Geometric spans the whole range depending on the palette.

Your surface
Text-heavy, denseVisual, immersive

Dense surfaces want Playful Geometric or none — anything heavier competes with the content. Immersive surfaces can carry 3D, Riso or Cut and Paste.

What you can actually make
No designerIllustrator on call

The honest axis. A style you cannot extend consistently becomes six mismatched drawings within a year, which is worse than none.

Commit to a system, not a look. The biggest mistake in illustration is mixing families without one. Pick a single primary style, one colour system shared with the brand palette, and one scale language — then, at most, one accent style used in a named context.

Consistency beats quality. Six consistent mediocre illustrations read as a system. Six excellent inconsistent ones read as clip art.
